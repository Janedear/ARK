How ARK connects people
In a crisis, ARK makes its own internet from the devices around you.
  1. One laptop becomes the hub.
  2. Nearby phones join it over Wi-Fi.
  3. Hubs pass messages on to each other, by network, radio, or a USB stick.

The network is local. It needs an ARK Hub running on a laptop or Raspberry Pi. A phone alone cannot be the hub. The more neighbors who have ARK, the more it helps, so pass it on.

Satellite relay (advanced)
ARK does not send anything by satellite itself. This is reference information. When all other networks fail, licensed amateur radio operators can relay short messages through amateur satellites and the ISS.
ISS APRS — the ISS relays short digital packets when it is overhead. No fee. Needs an amateur radio license.
AMSAT CubeSats — some support digital packet relay. See the Radio section.
iPhone satellite — Emergency SOS by satellite on iPhone 14 and newer. This is Apple's feature, separate from ARK.
Legal note: In the US, sending through amateur satellites needs an FCC amateur radio license. Encryption and commercial traffic are not allowed under Part 97.
