CPR Steps (Adult):
  1. Check scene safety. Tap shoulder: "Are you OK?"
  2. Call 911 (or have someone call). Note time.
  3. Lay on back, tilt head back, lift chin.
  4. Check breathing for no more than 10 seconds. Not breathing, or only gasping? Start CPR.
  5. Place heel of hand on center of chest (between nipples).
  6. Push hard, fast: at least 2 inches deep at 100–120/min.
  7. Allow full recoil between compressions.
  8. After 30 compressions, give 2 rescue breaths if trained. If you are not trained, skip the breaths and keep pushing without stopping.
  9. Continue until help arrives or AED is ready. If someone else can help, swap about every 2 minutes so you do not tire.
CPR Steps (Child 1–8 yrs):
  1. Check scene safety. Tap shoulder: "Are you OK?"
  2. Call 911 (or have someone call).
  3. Lay on back, tilt head back gently, lift chin.
  4. Check breathing for no more than 10 seconds. Not breathing, or only gasping? Start CPR.
  5. Use 1 or 2 hands on center of chest.
  6. Push about 2 inches deep at 100–120/min.
  7. Allow full recoil. 30 compressions then 2 rescue breaths.
  8. Rescue breath: pinch the nose, seal your mouth over the child's mouth; breathe gently until the chest rises.
  9. Continue until help arrives or child responds.
CPR Steps (Infant <1 yr):
  1. Flick foot gently — no response? Call 911.
  2. Lay on firm flat surface on back.
  3. Tilt head back slightly — do not hyperextend.
  4. Check breathing for no more than 10 seconds. Not breathing, or only gasping? Start CPR.
  5. Wrap both hands around the chest and press with both thumbs side by side on the center of the chest, just below the nipple line. If you cannot reach around, use the heel of one hand.
  6. Push about 1.5 inches deep at 100–120/min.
  7. After 30 compressions: cover mouth AND nose, give 2 small gentle puffs.
  8. Continue 30:2 until help arrives.
Choking / Heimlich Maneuver
Signs: Cannot speak, cough, or breathe. Blue lips. Hands at throat.
  1. Ask: "Are you choking?" If they nod: tell them you'll help. Have someone call 911.
  2. Stand behind them, one foot forward for stability.
  3. Bend them forward at the waist. Give 5 firm back blows between shoulder blades with heel of hand.
  4. If object not expelled: wrap arms around waist.
  5. Make a fist, place just above navel below breastbone.
  6. Cover fist with other hand. Give 5 sharp upward thrusts.
  7. Alternate 5 back blows + 5 abdominal thrusts until cleared or person loses consciousness.
  8. If unconscious: lower to floor, call 911, begin CPR.
If the person is pregnant or too large to reach around: give chest thrusts instead, with your fist on the center of the breastbone.

  1. Call 911 (or have someone call) immediately.
  2. Hold infant face-down on your forearm, head lower than body.
  3. Give 5 firm back blows between shoulder blades with heel of hand.
  4. Flip infant face-up on your forearm, head lower.
  5. Use the heel of one hand on the center of the chest (just below nipple line): give 5 chest thrusts.
  6. Check mouth for visible object — only remove if you can clearly see it.
  7. Alternate 5 back blows + 5 chest thrusts until cleared or infant becomes unconscious.
  8. If unconscious: begin infant CPR.
If you are alone and choking:
  1. Try to call 911 first if possible — stay on the line.
  2. Try to cough forcefully.
  3. Make a fist: place just above your navel.
  4. Cover with other hand; thrust sharply inward and upward.
  5. OR lean over a hard chair back or counter edge and thrust your upper abdomen against it.
  6. Repeat until object is dislodged.

Wound Care
  1. Wash hands or use gloves before touching a wound.
  2. Apply firm, continuous pressure with clean cloth — do not lift to check for 10 min.
  3. If cloth soaks through, add more on top. Don't remove.
  4. Elevate the injured area above heart level if possible.
  5. Once bleeding slows, clean wound with clean water under pressure.
  6. Cover with clean bandage. Change if it gets wet or dirty.
  7. Signs of infection (days later): increasing redness, warmth, swelling, pus, red streaks, fever — seek medical help.
For arterial bleeding from an arm or leg (bright red, spurting): apply tourniquet 2–3 inches above wound, not over a joint. Note the time. Do not remove. Get to medical help immediately.
