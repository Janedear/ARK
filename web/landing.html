How it connects

ARK makes its own internet from the devices around you.

No towers, no cables, no company in the middle. Just the phones and laptops that are already there.

Everyone has ARK

It lives on each person's phone as a small app that needs no signal. Guides, plans and the built-in guide work on their own.

One laptop becomes the hub

Any laptop runs the free ARK Hub. Every phone on its Wi-Fi joins in to post and read messages and to pick up ARK for themselves. A router with no internet is enough.

Hubs link up

Hubs pass messages on to each other — over any network that still works, by radio, or carried on a USB stick — so news travels street to street.

For official services

Emergency services and agencies: connect to ARK.

If you run emergency management, a responder team, a shelter, a utility or a community organisation, ask to link with ARK — to share verified local information, or to connect your own hub.

What ARK is — and is not

ARK is not an emergency service and nobody monitors it. In immediate danger, call 911 or your local emergency number.

The network ARK makes is local: it reaches as far as the hubs and Wi-Fi people set up, and its message board is public to everyone on it. Set up a hub and practise before you need it.

The guides draw on public sources such as the Red Cross, the American Heart Association, the CDC and FEMA. They are a reference, not a substitute for training or professional care.
